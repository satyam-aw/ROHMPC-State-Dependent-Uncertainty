\begin{abstract}
We study state-dependent process uncertainty in a continuous-time output-feedback tube MPC formulation. Starting from a fixed quadratic observer certificate, a Schur-complement construction reduces its auxiliary process envelope for nested local disturbance sets without changing the metric or gains. This yields coupled prediction equations for a squared estimation-error bound and an observer-to-nominal tracking radius. We state conditional recursive-feasibility and robust-constraint-satisfaction guarantees for a free-center MPC formulation, following the architecture of K\"ohler, M\"uller, and Allg\"ower. A Falcon numerical example reuses existing offline certificates and gives a 6.68\% final-time estimation-radius reduction relative to the baseline-equivalent global propagation. The experiment concerns radius dynamics under a prescribed speed schedule; it is not a closed-loop plant simulation. Continuous-domain certification, rigorous numerical residual treatment, local uncertainty validation, and a nonempty compatible terminal set remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Robust output-feedback MPC must account for both uncertain state estimates and deviations between an observer trajectory and a nominal prediction. A global uncertainty envelope can be unnecessarily conservative in operating regions with smaller model mismatch. Our objective is to retain an existing offline design while allowing the predicted uncertainty allowance to depend on the operating region covered by the error tubes.

The organization follows \cite{kohler2021}: preliminaries, estimation bounds, robust output-feedback MPC, and a numerical example. Joint nominal and tube prediction, a free nominal initial center, and shifted-candidate arguments are established ingredients of Section IV-B of that reference. They are not claimed as new here. Our proposed extension concerns the local process-envelope construction and its incorporation into coupled continuous-time radius dynamics.

We consider robust output-feedback MPC with fixed metrics and observer/controller gains, a fixed regulation target, and time-invariant constraints.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Plant, observer, and nominal prediction}
Consider
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:system}
\begin{align}
\dot x &= f_0(x,u)+E_0w_x, & w_x&\in\mathcal W(x,u),\\
y &= Cx+F\eta, &\eta&\in\mathcal N,\\
\dot{\hat x}&=f_0(\hat x,u)+L(y-C\hat x),\\
\dot z&=f_0(z,v), & u&=v+K(\hat x-z).
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here $x,\hat x,z\in\R^n$ and $u,v\in\R^{n_u}$; $v$ is the nominal control input, $E_0\in\R^{n\times m}$ maps process-disturbance components into the state dynamics, and $\mathcal W(x,u)\subset\R^m$ describes their admissible values at the current state and input. The physical process disturbance is $d=E_0w_x$. Measurement noise belongs to the fixed bounded set $\mathcal N$, and $L$ is a fixed observer gain. The nominal model $f_0$ includes any identified constant bias, so $w_x$ represents the centered residual uncertainty.

We assume a global baseline box encloses the process uncertainty on the operating region under consideration:
\[
\mathcal W(x,u)\subseteq\cW_0,
\qquad
\cW_0=\{w\in\R^m:|w_j|\le\bar w_j,\ j=1,\ldots,m\},\quad\bar w_j>0.
\]
The local sets $\mathcal W(x,u)$ specify the state--input dependence. Their containment in $\cW_0$ permits use of a global baseline certificate. Section III-B introduces the particular family of local sets used to obtain an explicit certificate reduction.
Let $P=P_\delta\succ0$, $X=P^{-1}$, and $T^\top T=P$. Define
\begin{equation}
e=x-\hat x,\quad \delta=\hat x-z,\quad V_e=e^\top Pe,\quad
V_e\le R,\quad \norm{\delta}_P\le s.
\end{equation}
$R$ is a squared radius and $r=\sqrt R$ is a radius; neither denotes the true estimation error. The symbol $P$ is distinct from a terminal-cost matrix. Since $x-z=e+\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
\norm{x-z}_P\le\sqrt R+s.
\end{equation}
Subtracting the dynamics gives
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:errors}
\begin{align}
\dot e&=f_0(x,u)-f_0(\hat x,u)-LCe+E_0w_x-LF\eta,\\
\dot\delta&=f_0(\hat x,u)-f_0(z,v)+LCe+LF\eta.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Process disturbance enters the estimation error directly. It influences observer-to-nominal tracking indirectly through $e$; the observer equation itself contains no additive plant process disturbance.

\subsection{Time and prediction notation}
Actual signals use a single time subscript, for example $x_t,\hat x_t,u_t$, with continuous physical time $t$. Plans are computed at sampling instants $t\in\{0,h,2h,\ldots\}$, where $h>0$ is the sampling period. Predicted trajectories use paired subscripts: $z_{\tau\mid t},v_{\tau\mid t},R_{\tau\mid t},s_{\tau\mid t}$ denote predictions computed at time $t$ for physical time $t+\tau$, with continuous prediction time $\tau\in[0,H_p]$ and horizon $H_p\ge h$. A dot denotes differentiation with respect to physical time for actual signals and with respect to $\tau$, holding $t$ fixed, for predictions. The next update occurs at $t+h$. The symbol $\bar R_0$ denotes the externally supplied initial squared estimation-error bound. Time subscripts are omitted in pointwise derivations when no time dependence needs to be displayed. Parentheses denote function evaluation, such as $f_0(x_t,u_t)$.
\subsection{Well-posedness}
\begin{assumption}[Well-posed model and observer]\label{ass:wellposed}
The nominal model $f_0$ is continuously differentiable in state and input. Given initial states and admissible locally bounded measurable disturbance and control signals, the plant, observer, and nominal dynamics admit unique absolutely continuous solutions over the intervals considered.
\end{assumption}
This assumption ensures that the state trajectories used in the error analysis are uniquely defined for the specified initial conditions and admissible signals.
\section{State-Dependent Estimation Error Bounds}
\subsection{Recovering the global quadratic certificate}
The starting point is the offline robust output-feedback design of Benders et al.~\cite{benders2025}. Let $\epsilon\ge0$ denote the constant estimation-error radius supplied by its global observer certificate, expressed here in the $P$-norm:
\[
\norm{x-\hat x}_P\le\epsilon,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad V_e\le\epsilon^2.
\]
This invariant bound holds provided $V_{e,0}\le\epsilon^2$ and the certificate assumptions hold. We use the observer-certificate LMIs implemented in the accompanying ROHMPC repository~\cite{rohmpc}, specialized here to a constant metric and written in the notation of this manuscript. This subsection recovers the original constant radius $\epsilon$ and introduces a time-varying squared radius $R_{t}$; Section III-B then constructs the local process-envelope reduction.
For $b\in\R^n$, introduce the symmetric off-diagonal block
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S(b)=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\b^\top&0\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Let $A_o=A-LC$ and $q_0=\lambda\epsilon^2$, with $\lambda>0$. Let $W$ and $H$ be symmetric process and noise envelope matrices, respectively. The following observer-certificate LMIs are taken from the ROHMPC implementation accompanying Benders et al.~\cite{benders2025,rohmpc}, specialized to a constant metric and expressed in the present notation. They are sufficient design conditions for $\dot V_e+\lambda V_e\le q_0$:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:lmis}
\begin{align}
\mathcal S(E_0w)&\preceq W &&\forall w\in\cW_0,\label{eq:baseline-process}\\
\mathcal S(-LF\eta)&\preceq H &&\forall\eta\in\mathcal N,\label{eq:baseline-noise}\\
W+H+\begin{bmatrix}XA_o^\top+A_oX+\lambda X&0\\0&-q_0\end{bmatrix}&\preceq0.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here $A=\partial f_0/\partial x$. The last inequality is a condition on the state Jacobians, and must hold along the segments joining the true and estimated states at their common input. Since $X$ is constant, its time derivative is zero.

To see explicitly how these LMIs imply the error bound, first use the global baseline uncertainty $w_x\in\cW_0$, writing $w=w_x$. Equation~\eqref{eq:errors} can be written as
\[
\dot e=A_oe+b,\qquad b:=E_0w-LF\eta.
\]
For the nonlinear model, the matrix in the error dynamics is the segment average
\[
A_o=\int_0^1\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial x}(\hat x+\theta e,u)\,d\theta-LC.
\]
Indeed, integrating the state Jacobian along the segment gives $f_0(x,u)-f_0(\hat x,u)=(A_o+LC)e$. Since the dynamics LMI is affine in the Jacobian, its validity along the segment implies its validity for this average. The vector $b$ collects the process disturbance and measurement-noise contributions.

Rewrite the first two inequalities in \eqref{eq:lmis} as $\mathcal S(E_0w)-W\preceq0$ and $\mathcal S(-LF\eta)-H\preceq0$, and add the third. The auxiliary matrices cancel:
\[
\underbrace{\mathcal S(E_0w)-W}_{\preceq0}
+\underbrace{\mathcal S(-LF\eta)-H}_{\preceq0}
+\underbrace{W+H+\diag(XA_o^\top+A_oX+\lambda X,-q_0)}_{\preceq0}
\preceq0.
\]
Since $\mathcal S$ is linear,
\[
\mathcal S(E_0w)+\mathcal S(-LF\eta)=\mathcal S(b),
\]
so the resulting matrix inequality is
\[
M:=\begin{bmatrix}
XA_o^\top+A_oX+\lambda X&b\\
b^\top&-q_0
\end{bmatrix}\preceq0.
\]
Negative semidefiniteness means $\xi^\top M\xi\le0$ for every vector $\xi$. Choosing $\xi=[Pe;1]$ and using $PX=XP=I$ and constant $P$ gives
\begin{align*}
\begin{bmatrix}Pe\\1\end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}M\begin{bmatrix}Pe\\1\end{bmatrix}
&=e^\top P(XA_o^\top+A_oX+\lambda X)Pe+2e^\top Pb-q_0\\
&=e^\top(A_o^\top P+PA_o)e+2e^\top Pb+\lambda e^\top Pe-q_0\\
&=\dot V_e+\lambda V_e-q_0\le0.
\end{align*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-dissipation}
\dot V_e\le-\lambda V_e+q_0.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, define the invertible matrix $G=\diag(P,1)$. The congruence transformation of $M$ by $G$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observer-congruence}
G^\top MG=
\begin{bmatrix}A_o^\top P+PA_o+\lambda P&Pb\\b^\top P&-q_0\end{bmatrix}\preceq0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $v^\top G^\top MGv=(Gv)^\top M(Gv)\le0$ for every $v$; invertibility makes the two matrix inequalities equivalent. Evaluating the transformed matrix at $[e;1]$ is identical to evaluating $M$ at $[Pe;1]$, because $G[e;1]=[Pe;1]$. These inequalities hold for every admissible $w,\eta$.

To bound the estimation error, introduce a scalar squared radius $R_{t}$ defining the time-varying error set
\[
\mathcal E_{t}=\{e:e^\top Pe\le R_{t}\}.
\]
Thus $R_{t}$ is the upper boundary in the scalar $V_e$ coordinate, and $e^\top Pe=R_{t}$ is the corresponding boundary in error-state space. Choose $R_{0}\ge V_{e,0}$ and propagate this boundary using the comparison equation, whose solution bounds $V_e$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-global}
\dot R=-\lambda R+q_0.
\end{equation}
Subtracting the comparison equation~\eqref{eq:comparison-global} from~\eqref{eq:global-dissipation} gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(V_e-R)\le-\lambda(V_e-R),
\qquad
V_{e,t}-R_{t}\le e^{-\lambda t}\bigl(V_{e,0}-R_{0}\bigr)\le0.
\]
Consequently, $V_{e,t}\le R_{t}$ and $\sqrt{R_{t}}$ bounds the estimation error in the $P$-norm wherever the derivative inequality holds. The comparison equation~\eqref{eq:comparison-global} has solution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-solution}
R_{t}=\epsilon^2+(R_{0}-\epsilon^2)e^{-\lambda t}.
\end{equation}
When $R_{0}\ge V_{e,0}$ it bounds the estimation error; when $R_{0}=\epsilon^2$, it exactly recovers the original constant radius $\epsilon$.

\subsection{Local process-envelope reduction}\label{sec:local-envelope}
The objective is to replace the global observer forcing $q_0$ by a smaller allowance that remains valid for the local process set. Starting from the model $d=E_0w_x$, $w_x\in\mathcal W(x,u)$, we now specialize to a family parameterized by a scalar amplitude:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scheduled-set}
\mathcal W(x,u)=\mathcal W(x)=D(a(x))\cW_0,
\qquad \beta\le a(x)\le1,\quad 0<\beta<1.
\end{equation}
Thus $w_x=D(a(x))w$ for some $w\in\cW_0$. Choose a fixed subset $\mathcal J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ identifying the disturbance components assumed to vary with the operating state; the remaining components retain their global bounds. This is a modeling choice, and selecting all components is permitted. Define
\[
D(a)=\diag(d_1(a),\ldots,d_m(a)),\qquad
 d_j(a)=\begin{cases}a,&j\in\mathcal J,\\1,&j\notin\mathcal J.\end{cases}
\]
This construction is affine in $a$, has $D(1)=I$, and produces nested sets:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:set-nesting}
\beta\le a_1\le a_2\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_0D(a_1)\cW_0\subseteq E_0D(a_2)\cW_0.
\end{equation}
The inclusion follows from the centered box bounds $|w_j|\le\bar w_j$: increasing $a$ enlarges the bounds of the selected components while leaving the others fixed. It remains valid after applying $E_0$.

The scalar structure serves two purposes. Affine dependence of $D(a)$ makes the vertex quadratics defined before~\eqref{eq:vertex-maximum} convex in $a$, allowing a bound based on the two endpoint values. Nestedness permits an upper amplitude bound to enclose disturbances at all true states in a predicted tube. A general state-dependent matrix $D(x)$ can also define a disturbance model, but these properties need not hold: one would instead need to bound its process envelope over the whole tube, and the endpoint formula derived here would not follow automatically. Equation~\eqref{eq:scheduled-set} specifies the structure used in this construction; the velocity law in Section III-C is one choice of $a(x)$.
Let $W\in\mathbb S^{n+1}$ satisfy the baseline process-envelope inequality~\eqref{eq:baseline-process}, where $\mathbb S^{n+1}$ denotes the symmetric $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrices. Introduce block notation for this envelope matrix:
\begin{equation}
W=\begin{bmatrix}Q&h_w\\h_w^\top&c\end{bmatrix},\qquad Q\succ0.
\end{equation}
Here $Q\in\mathbb S^n$ is the upper-left block, $h_w\in\R^n$ is the off-diagonal block, and $c\in\R$ is the bottom-right entry. These are fixed blocks of the global baseline envelope matrix $W$; the subscript in $h_w$ does not indicate dependence on the disturbance $w$. The local construction assumes $Q\succ0$, keeps $Q$ and $h_w$ fixed, and reduces the scalar entry $c$ to accommodate smaller disturbance sets.

Taking the Schur complement with respect to $Q\succ0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur}
\mathcal S(E_0D(a)w)\preceq W
\iff (E_0D(a)w-h_w)^\top Q^{-1}(E_0D(a)w-h_w)\le c.
\end{equation}
The matrix $Q^{-1}$ determines the quadratic weighting, $h_w$ its center, and $c$ the admissible level. The box $\cW_0$ has $2^m$ vertices $w^{(i)}$, obtained by choosing each component as $\pm\bar w_j$. Define
\[
\phi(w,a):=(E_0D(a)w-h_w)^\top Q^{-1}(E_0D(a)w-h_w),
\qquad \phi_i(a):=\phi(w^{(i)},a).
\]
Here $\phi_i$ is the expression at the fixed vertex $w^{(i)}$, regarded as a function of the scale $a$. Their pointwise maximum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-maximum}
g(a)=\max_{i=1,\ldots,2^m}\phi_i(a)
=\max_{w\in\operatorname{vert}(\cW_0)}\phi(w,a).
\end{equation}
For fixed $a$, the Hessian with respect to $w$ is
\[
\nabla_w^2\phi(w,a)=2D(a)^\top E_0^\top Q^{-1}E_0D(a)\succeq0,
\]
so $\phi(\cdot,a)$ is convex. Any $w\in\cW_0$ can be written as $w=\sum_i\theta_iw^{(i)}$ with $\theta_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i\theta_i=1$. Therefore,
\[
\phi(w,a)\le\sum_i\theta_i\phi(w^{(i)},a)\le\max_i\phi_i(a)=g(a).
\]
Since every box point is a convex combination of its vertices and the vertices belong to the box, $g(a)=\max_{w\in\cW_0}\phi(w,a)$. Thus the vertex maximum bounds the entire disturbance box, including its interior.

For a fixed vertex, the affine dependence of $D(a)$ permits the representation
\[
E_0D(a)w^{(i)}-h_w=a p_i+r_i,
\qquad
\phi_i''(a)=2p_i^\top Q^{-1}p_i\ge0,
\]
where $p_i,r_i$ are fixed vectors. Each vertex function is consequently convex in $a$, and their pointwise maximum $g$ is convex as well, although it need not be differentiable where the maximizing vertex changes. Nestedness of $E_0D(a)\cW_0$ also gives $g(\beta)\le g(1)$.

A convex function lies below the chord joining its endpoint values. Writing
$a=\frac{1-a}{1-\beta}\beta+\frac{a-\beta}{1-\beta}\,1$ for $a\in[\beta,1]$ yields
\[
g(a)\le\frac{1-a}{1-\beta}g(\beta)+\frac{a-\beta}{1-\beta}g(1).
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord}
g(a)\le g(1)-\frac{1-a}{1-\beta}\bigl(g(1)-g(\beta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $\beta<a<1$, the same inequality can be expressed as a comparison of secant slopes:
\[
\frac{g(1)-g(a)}{1-a}\ge\frac{g(1)-g(\beta)}{1-\beta}.
\]
The secant ending at $1$ and starting at $a$ has slope at least as large as the secant from $\beta$ to $1$. Hence the chord provides a guaranteed reduction from the global value $g(1)$, without requiring derivatives of $g$. The following proposition transfers this reduction to the process-envelope entry $c$ and the observer forcing $q_0$ by reducing both by the same amount.
\begin{proposition}[Local forcing from a fixed certificate]\label{prop:local}
Suppose the baseline certificate~\eqref{eq:lmis} holds exactly, $Q\succ0$, and the local disturbance sets have the structure~\eqref{eq:scheduled-set}. Choose
$0\le\Delta\le g(1)-g(\beta)$ and define
\begin{align}
\theta(a)&=\frac{1-a}{1-\beta},\\
W(a)&=\begin{bmatrix}Q&h_w\\h_w^\top&c-\Delta\theta(a)\end{bmatrix},\\
q(a)&=q_0-\Delta\theta(a).\label{eq:q}
\end{align}
For every $a\in[\beta,1]$, the reduced envelope satisfies $\mathcal S(E_0D(a)w)\preceq W(a)$ for all $w\in\cW_0$. Together with the unchanged noise envelope, this gives the derivative bound
$\dot V_e\le-\lambda V_e+q(a)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $a=1$, $D(1)=I$. The process-envelope inequality~\eqref{eq:baseline-process} of the global baseline certificate, together with the Schur-complement equivalence~\eqref{eq:schur} and the vertex-maximum identity~\eqref{eq:vertex-maximum}, implies
\[
c\ge\max_{w\in\cW_0}\phi(w,1)=\max_i\phi_i(1)=g(1).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:chord} and the choice of $\Delta$ give $g(a)\le c-\Delta\theta(a)$, proving the local envelope inequality by \eqref{eq:schur}. To retain the dynamics certificate, reduce $q_0$ by the same amount as the bottom-right entry of $W$. Indeed, writing $B_o=XA_o^\top+A_oX+\lambda X$ gives
\[
W(a)+H+\diag(B_o,-q(a))
=W+H+\diag(B_o,-q_0)\preceq0.
\]
Thus the disturbance envelope can shrink while the dynamics LMI remains identical. Adding the reduced process-envelope inequality and the unchanged noise-envelope inequality~\eqref{eq:baseline-noise}, together with this dynamics inequality, yields $\dot V_e\le-\lambda V_e+q(a)$ by the quadratic-form calculation in Section III-A.
\end{proof}
At $a=1$, $W(a)=W$ and $q(a)=q_0$. For smaller $a$, the chord bound gives a guaranteed process-envelope reduction, which is transferred to the observer forcing without changing the metric or gains. The construction therefore follows the sequence
\[
\mathcal W(x)=D(a(x))\cW_0
\ \longrightarrow\ g(a)\ \longrightarrow\ W(a)\ \longrightarrow\ q(a).
\]
Here $\mathcal W(x)$ is a set of admissible disturbance components, $E_0w_x$ is the physical disturbance, $W(a)$ is its auxiliary matrix envelope, and $q(a)$ is the scalar forcing allowance in the estimation-error inequality. For use in MPC, $x$ is unknown. We must next bound $a(x)$ over the predicted error tube so that the same local forcing is valid for every true state represented by that tube. For the positive-forcing formulation below, choose $\Delta$ such that $q_0-\Delta>0$.
\subsection{Velocity-Dependent Disturbance Bound over the Predicted Tube}\label{sec:velocity-envelope}
Let $S_v$ select the translational velocity components of the state, and denote the true velocity vector by $v_x:=S_vx$. The symbol $v$ in the nominal dynamics continues to denote the nominal control input.
Consider the velocity-dependent amplitude law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:velocity-amplitude}
a(x)=\beta+(1-\beta)\frac{\norm{S_vx}_2^2}{v_{\max}^2},\qquad
\norm{S_vx}_2\le v_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The amplitude enters the physical process disturbance through
\[
d(x,w)=E_0D(a(x))w,\qquad w\in\cW_0.
\]
The matrix $D(a)$ is the component scaling defined in Section~\ref{sec:local-envelope}. Thus a bound on true speed determines an enclosing local process-disturbance set.

Recall that $T^\top T=P$, so $\norm e_P=\norm{Te}_2$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:velocity-metric-factor}
c_v:=\norm{S_vT^{-1}}_2.
\end{equation}
Using the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the induced matrix norm bound, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\norm{S_ve}_2
&=\norm{S_vT^{-1}(Te)}_2\\
&\le\norm{S_vT^{-1}}_2\norm{Te}_2
=c_v\norm e_P\le c_v\sqrt R.
\end{align*}
The same argument gives $\norm{S_v\delta}_2\le c_v\norm\delta_P\le c_vs$.
Starting from the state decomposition $x=z+\delta+e$, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{align*}
\norm{S_vx}_2
&=\norm{S_vz+S_v\delta+S_ve}_2\\
&\le\norm{S_vz}_2+\norm{S_v\delta}_2+\norm{S_ve}_2\\
&\le\norm{S_vz}_2+c_v(s+\sqrt R).
\end{align*}
Combining this tube bound with the certified domain bound $\norm{S_vx}_2\le v_{\max}$ defines
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-speed-bound}
v_{\rm up}:=\min\{v_{\max},\norm{S_vz}_2+c_v(\sqrt R+s)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus $v_{\rm up}$ bounds true speed for every state in the predicted error tube that lies in the certified domain. The cap $v_{\max}$ requires domain containment of the true-state tube; a bound on the nominal speed alone does not establish it.

Since the amplitude law~\eqref{eq:velocity-amplitude} is nondecreasing in speed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-amplitude-bound}
a(x)\le a_{\rm up}:=\beta+(1-\beta)(v_{\rm up}/v_{\max})^2.
\end{equation}
The set inclusion~\eqref{eq:set-nesting} then gives
\[
E_0D(a(x))\cW_0\subseteq E_0D(a_{\rm up})\cW_0.
\]
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:local} to this enclosing set yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-observer-bound}
\dot V_e\le-\lambda V_e+q(a_{\rm up}),\qquad
q(z,R,s):=q(a_{\rm up}).
\end{equation}
More explicitly, the scalar used in the estimation-radius dynamics is
\[
q(z,R,s)=q_0-\Delta\frac{1-a_{\rm up}(z,R,s)}{1-\beta}.
\]
The local uncertainty model specifies $a(x)$, while the predicted state and radii determine its enclosing value $a_{\rm up}$. The resulting $q(z,R,s)$ is therefore a bound valid throughout that tube. Here $q$ has no explicit dependence on the nominal input $v$. Section IV writes $q(z,v,R,s)$ for the general case.
\subsection{Comparison with Separate-Norm Disturbance Bounds}
Retaining the model, metric, and gains from Section II and the uncertainty sets from Section III-B, consider an alternative estimation-error bound obtained by bounding the process-disturbance and measurement-noise contributions separately. Derivative inequalities for error norms are understood to hold almost everywhere in time. If the disturbance-free observer error satisfies $\frac{d}{dt}\norm e_P\le-\rho_o\norm e_P$ for a contraction rate $\rho_o>0$, a radius bound can be propagated as
\begin{equation}
\dot r=-\rho_o r+b_{\rm rest}+a_{\rm up}b_{\rm acc}+b_\eta.
\end{equation}
Here $b_{\rm acc}=\max\norm{TE_{\rm acc}w_{\rm acc}}_2$ bounds the unscaled acceleration-disturbance contribution, $b_{\rm rest}$ bounds the remaining process contribution, and $b_\eta=\max_{\eta\in\mathcal N}\norm{TLF\eta}_2$ bounds measurement noise. Each maximum is taken over its corresponding admissible set.

For vector contributions $d_j(\omega_j)$ with $\omega_j\in\Omega_j$, this construction uses
\[
\max_{\omega\in\prod_j\Omega_j}\left\|\sum_j d_j(\omega_j)\right\|_P
\le\sum_j\max_{\omega_j\in\Omega_j}\norm{d_j(\omega_j)}_P.
\]
The triangle inequality and separate maximizations discard directional information: the individual largest contributions need not align to attain the sum of their norms. Thus this approach can be more conservative and need not recover the original SDP radius at $a=1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:local} retains the original quadratic certificate and recovers its global baseline at $a=1$.
\section{Robust Output-Feedback MPC}
\subsection{Coupled tube dynamics}
Suppose the tracking contraction condition holds along every state--input segment joining $(z,v)$ to $(\hat x,v+K(\hat x-z))$ in the certified domain:
\begin{equation}
(A+BK)^\top P+P(A+BK)\preceq-2\rho P,\qquad\rho>0.\label{eq:tracking-contraction}
\end{equation}
With $T^\top T=P$, define
\[
\gamma:=\norm{TLC T^{-1}}_2,\qquad
b_\eta:=\max_{\eta\in\mathcal N}\norm{TLF\eta}_2.
\]
The constant $b_\eta$ bounds the measurement-noise contribution $LF\eta$ to the observer dynamics in the $P$-norm. Equation~\eqref{eq:errors} then implies
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\norm\delta_P\le-\rho\norm\delta_P+\gamma\sqrt{V_e}+b_\eta.\label{eq:tracking-bound}
\end{equation}
The derivation of \eqref{eq:tracking-bound}, using the contraction condition~\eqref{eq:tracking-contraction} and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, is given in Appendix~\ref{app:tracking-bound}. Thus the proposed augmented prediction is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:tubes}
\begin{align}
\dot z&=f_0(z,v),\\
\dot R&=-\lambda R+q(z,v,R,s),\\
\dot s&=-\rho s+\gamma\sqrt R+b_\eta.\label{eq:tracking-radius}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The general notation permits input dependence in $q$; the present velocity example has none explicitly. The forcing bound $q(z,v,R,s)$ must be valid for every state--input pair represented by the predicted tube and every admissible disturbance realization.

\begin{assumption}[Certificate validity on the operating domain]\label{ass:domain}
Let $\cD\subseteq\R^n\times\R^{n_u}$ be a specified state--input region. The baseline observer LMIs~\eqref{eq:lmis} and tracking contraction condition~\eqref{eq:tracking-contraction} hold on a neighborhood of $\cD$. The disturbance-set model is valid there. For any predicted $(z,v,R,s)$ and errors satisfying $e^\top Pe\le R$ and $\norm\delta_P\le s$, set $\hat x=z+\delta$, $x=z+\delta+e$, and $u=v+K\delta$. The certificate applies whenever the segments
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\{(\hat x+\theta e,u):0\le\theta\le1\},\\
&\{(z+\theta\delta,v+\theta K\delta):0\le\theta\le1\}
\end{aligned}
\]
lie in $\cD$. The scalar forcing allowance $q(z,v,R,s)$ bounds the contribution of $E_0w_x-LF\eta$ to the estimation-error inequality for every such true state and input, with $w_x\in\mathcal W(x,u)$ and $\eta\in\mathcal N$. Section III-C constructs this allowance for the velocity-dependent example. Under these conditions, the derivations in Sections III and IV-A give the estimation-error inequality~\eqref{eq:local-observer-bound}, with the forcing allowance $q(z,v,R,s)$, and the tracking-error inequality~\eqref{eq:tracking-bound}.
\end{assumption}
The two segments connect true and estimated states at a common input, and nominal and estimated states with the feedback input, respectively. To keep these certificates applicable, the MPC imposes the domain-containment condition that both segments lie in $\cD$ for every error pair in its predicted tube. This is a constraint on the prediction, distinct from the certificate inequalities and from the physical state--input constraints.
\begin{assumption}[Admissible radius box and initial bounds]\label{ass:radii}
The forcing bound $q(z,v,R,s)$ is locally Lipschitz in its arguments on the set of admissible nominal states, inputs, and radius pairs, and satisfies
$0<q_{\min}\le q\le q_{\max}$. Define
\begin{equation}
0<R_{\min}\le q_{\min}/\lambda,\quad R_{\max}=q_{\max}/\lambda,\quad
s_{\max}=(\gamma\sqrt{R_{\max}}+b_\eta)/\rho.
\end{equation}
The pair of radius variables $(R,s)$ is constrained to the rectangle
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B&=[R_{\min},R_{\max}]\times[0,s_{\max}]\\
&=\{(R,s):R_{\min}\le R\le R_{\max},\ 0\le s\le s_{\max}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These variables bound the errors through $e^\top Pe\le R$ and $\norm\delta_P\le s$. An available initial prior $\bar R_0\in[R_{\min},R_{\max}]$ satisfies $V_{e,0}\le\bar R_0$. The observer state evolves continuously according to the observer dynamics in Section II, including across MPC updates.
\end{assumption}
The upper radii provide uniform error margins for the terminal construction; the positive lower radius ensures regularity of the square-root term. Local Lipschitz continuity gives a well-posed radius evolution. Boundedness follows separately from invariance of $\mathcal B$, while actual-error containment is established in Lemma~\ref{lem:tube}. The coupled radius equations~\eqref{eq:tubes} satisfy the following conditions on the four boundaries of $\mathcal B$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R=R_{\min}:\quad &\dot R\ge-\lambda R_{\min}+q_{\min}\ge0,\\
R=R_{\max}:\quad &\dot R\le-\lambda R_{\max}+q_{\max}=0,\\
s=0:\quad &\dot s=\gamma\sqrt R+b_\eta\ge0,\\
s=s_{\max}:\quad &\dot s\le-\rho s_{\max}+\gamma\sqrt{R_{\max}}+b_\eta=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the radius pair cannot leave this rectangle along admissible nominal trajectories: if $(R_{0},s_{0})\in\mathcal B$, then $(R_{t},s_{t})\in\mathcal B$ for all subsequent times on which the assumptions hold. Since $\frac{d}{dR}\sqrt R=\frac{1}{2\sqrt R}$, the derivative is unbounded as $R\to0^+$. Restricting $R\ge R_{\min}>0$ bounds this derivative and ensures that the square-root term is Lipschitz on the admissible interval.

\begin{lemma}[Tube containment]\label{lem:tube}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:wellposed}--\ref{ass:radii}, consider a prediction satisfying the domain-containment conditions of Section IV-B throughout an applied interval. The estimation-error inequality~\eqref{eq:local-observer-bound} and tracking-error inequality~\eqref{eq:tracking-bound}, together with initial bounds $V_{e,t}\le R_{0\mid t}$ and $\norm{\hat x_t-z_{0\mid t}}_P\le s_{0\mid t}$, imply
\[
V_{e,t+\tau}\le R_{\tau\mid t},\qquad
\norm{\hat x_{t+\tau}-z_{\tau\mid t}}_P\le s_{\tau\mid t},
\qquad 0\le\tau\le h.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $V_e=R$, the derivative of $V_e-R$ is nonpositive. Subtracting \eqref{eq:tracking-radius} from \eqref{eq:tracking-bound} at $\norm\delta_P=s$, with $V_e\le R$, gives
$\frac{d}{dt}(\norm\delta_P-s)\le\gamma(\sqrt{V_e}-\sqrt R)\le0$.
While the errors lie within the predicted tube, the domain-containment constraints keep the relevant state--input segments inside the certificate domain, so the observer and tracking derivative inequalities remain valid. The comparison argument under the stated regularity conditions prevents an outward crossing of either error boundary. Thus containment persists throughout the applied interval.
\end{proof}
For identical nominal trajectories and initial radii, the local estimation-error forcing bound $q_{\rm local,t}\le q_0$, where $q_0=\lambda\epsilon^2$ is the global baseline forcing bound, yields $R_{\rm local,t}\le R_{\rm global,t}$ and $s_{\rm local,t}\le s_{\rm global,t}$. Thus the local formulation produces no larger predicted error bounds under these common conditions. The derivation is given in Appendix~\ref{app:radius-comparison}.

\subsection{Tightened constraints and MPC initialization}
For each state--input constraint $g_j(x,u)\le0$, impose the worst-case condition over the predicted error tube:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tightening}
\sup_{\norm e_P\le\sqrt R,\ \norm\delta_P\le s}
 g_j(z+\delta+e,v+K\delta)\le0.
\end{equation}
For an affine constraint $a_j^\top x+b_j^\top u\le c_j$, substituting $x=z+\delta+e$ and $u=v+K\delta$ gives the uncertain contribution $(a_j^\top+b_j^\top K)\delta+a_j^\top e$. The error balls have radii $s$ and $\sqrt R$, respectively. With $T^\top T=P$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives $\max_{\|T\xi\|_2\le r} a^\top\xi=r\|a^\top T^{-1}\|_2$; the bound is attained in the maximizing direction. Since the two errors range independently over their balls, their maxima add. Define the tightening constants
\[
c_{s,j}:=\norm{(a_j^\top+b_j^\top K)T^{-1}}_2,
\qquad c_{e,j}:=\norm{a_j^\top T^{-1}}_2.
\]
For fixed affine constraints, metric $P$, and gain $K$, these constants can be computed offline. The resulting tightened constraint is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-tightening}
a_j^\top z+b_j^\top v
+\underbrace{c_{s,j}s}_{\text{tracking-error margin}}
+\underbrace{c_{e,j}\sqrt R}_{\text{estimation-error margin}}
\le c_j.
\end{equation}
This expression evaluates the supremum exactly over the chosen product of estimation and tracking error balls. The margins vary along the prediction through $R$ and $s$.
Using the time notation in Section II, the tightened state--input constraints~\eqref{eq:tightening} (or~\eqref{eq:affine-tightening} for affine constraints), certificate-domain conditions in Assumption~\ref{ass:domain}, and radius bounds $(R_{\tau\mid t},s_{\tau\mid t})\in\mathcal B$ are imposed for every $\tau\in[0,H_p]$.
At update $t$, minimize the predicted horizon cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
J_t=\int_0^{H_p}\ell(z_{\tau\mid t},v_{\tau\mid t})\,d\tau+V_f(z_{H_p\mid t}),
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the stage-cost rate, its integral is the accumulated running cost, and $V_f$ is the terminal cost. Initialize $R_{0\mid 0}=\bar R_0$ using Assumption~\ref{ass:radii}. At subsequent updates, retain the previous plan's propagated bound:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius-update}
R_{0\mid t+h}=R_{h\mid t}.
\end{equation} The prediction satisfies~\eqref{eq:tubes}, the constraints above, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:init}
\norm{\hat x_t-z_{0\mid t}}_P\le s_{0\mid t},\quad
(z_{H_p\mid t},R_{H_p\mid t},s_{H_p\mid t})\in\Omega_f.
\end{equation}
The initial nominal center $z_{0\mid t}$ is free. For the selected feasible plan, apply
\[
u_{t+\tau}=v_{\tau\mid t}+K\bigl(\hat x_{t+\tau}-z_{\tau\mid t}\bigr),\qquad 0\le\tau<h,
\]
and initialize the next prediction with $R_{0\mid t+h}=R_{h\mid t}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tube} ensures $V_{e,t+h}\le R_{h\mid t}$, so this remains a valid estimation-error bound. Starting from an initially feasible MPC problem, recursive feasibility and constraint satisfaction hold when the controller applies a feasible plan at each update; optimality is not required for these guarantees. Superscript $*$ denotes optimal plans and the minimum horizon cost in the cost-decrease inequality~\eqref{eq:decrease} of Theorem~\ref{thm:mpc}.
\subsection{Terminal Ingredients and Recursive Feasibility}
\begin{assumption}[Terminal ingredients]\label{ass:terminal}
There exist a nonempty nominal terminal set $Z_f$ and controller $\kappa_f$ such that $\dot z=f_0(z,\kappa_f(z))$ leaves $Z_f$ invariant. For every $z\in Z_f$, with nominal input $v=\kappa_f(z)$, the tightened state--input constraints~\eqref{eq:tightening} and the certificate-domain conditions in Assumption~\ref{ass:domain} hold at $R=R_{\max}$ and $s=s_{\max}$. For the cost assertion additionally assume $\ell,V_f\ge0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminalcost}
\nabla V_f(z)f_0(z,\kappa_f(z))\le-\ell(z,\kappa_f(z)).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
Then $\Omega_f=Z_f\times\mathcal B$ is invariant under terminal control and the radius dynamics. The terminal set is certified using the largest admissible estimation and tracking radii. Since the smaller error balls are contained in these largest ones, this ensures terminal constraint satisfaction and certificate-domain containment for every $(R,s)\in\mathcal B$, while the predictions along the horizon retain their local, time-varying radii.

\begin{theorem}[Recursive Feasibility and Robust Constraint Satisfaction]\label{thm:mpc}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:wellposed}--\ref{ass:terminal} hold and the initial MPC problem is feasible. Applying a feasible plan satisfying~\eqref{eq:init} and initializing $R_{0\mid0}=\bar R_0$ and $R_{0\mid t+h}=R_{h\mid t}$ at subsequent updates gives recursive feasibility and continuous-time robust constraint satisfaction for all disturbances in the assumed sets. If \eqref{eq:terminalcost} holds and the selected feasible plans attain the minimum horizon cost among all feasible plans, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decrease}
J_{t+h}^*\le J_t^*-\int_0^h\ell(z_{\tau\mid t}^*,v_{\tau\mid t}^*)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leavevmode\par\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Part 1: Recursive feasibility.}\par\nobreak\smallskip\noindent
Suppose the plan $(z_{\tau\mid t},v_{\tau\mid t},R_{\tau\mid t},s_{\tau\mid t})$ is feasible at update $t$. For $0\le\tau\le H_p-h$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
z^c_{\tau\mid t+h}&=z_{\tau+h\mid t}, &\qquad v^c_{\tau\mid t+h}&=v_{\tau+h\mid t},\\
R^c_{\tau\mid t+h}&=R_{\tau+h\mid t}, & s^c_{\tau\mid t+h}&=s_{\tau+h\mid t}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Evaluating the shifted-candidate definition at $\tau=0$ gives
\[
z^c_{0\mid t+h}=z_{h\mid t},\qquad
R^c_{0\mid t+h}=R_{h\mid t},\qquad
s^c_{0\mid t+h}=s_{h\mid t}.
\]
The estimation-radius update rule~\eqref{eq:radius-update} sets $R_{0\mid t+h}=R_{h\mid t}$, so the candidate has the required initial radius $R^c_{0\mid t+h}=R_{0\mid t+h}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tube}, applied over the previous control interval, ensures $V_{e,t+h}\le R_{h\mid t}$ and
\[
\norm{\hat x_{t+h}-z_{h\mid t}}_P\le s_{h\mid t}.
\]
Using the shifted initial center and tracking radius therefore yields
\[
\norm{\hat x_{t+h}-z^c_{0\mid t+h}}_P
=\norm{\hat x_{t+h}-z_{h\mid t}}_P
\le s_{h\mid t}=s^c_{0\mid t+h}.
\]
Thus the candidate satisfies the prescribed estimation-radius initialization and the initial tracking bound in~\eqref{eq:init}. On this shifted interval the candidate reproduces the previous feasible trajectory. The coupled nominal and tube dynamics~\eqref{eq:tubes} and prediction constraints have no explicit time dependence. Consequently, the shifted candidate continues to satisfy those dynamics, the tightened constraints~\eqref{eq:tightening}, certificate-domain conditions, and radius bounds.

At $\tau=H_p-h$, the candidate reaches the previous terminal point in $\Omega_f$. Over the remaining $h$ units of time, apply $v^c_{\tau\mid t+h}=\kappa_f(z^c_{\tau\mid t+h})$ and continue the nominal and radius dynamics. Terminal invariance under Assumption~\ref{ass:terminal} and invariance of $\mathcal B$ keep this continuation in $\Omega_f$, where all tightened and domain constraints hold. Its endpoint therefore satisfies the new terminal condition. This constructs a feasible plan at update $t+h$; induction from initial feasibility proves recursive feasibility.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part 2: Robust constraint satisfaction.}\par\nobreak\smallskip\noindent
During each applied interval, Lemma~\ref{lem:tube} gives $\norm e_P\le\sqrt{R_{\tau\mid t}}$ and $\norm\delta_P\le s_{\tau\mid t}$. The actual pair satisfies $x_{t+\tau}=z_{\tau\mid t}+\delta+e$ and $u_{t+\tau}=v_{\tau\mid t}+K\delta$, and hence belongs to the set over which~\eqref{eq:tightening} takes its supremum. Consequently,
\[
g_j(x_{t+\tau},u_{t+\tau})\le
\sup_{\norm e_P\le\sqrt{R_{\tau\mid t}},\ \norm\delta_P\le s_{\tau\mid t}}
g_j(z_{\tau\mid t}+\delta+e,v_{\tau\mid t}+K\delta)\le0.
\]
This holds throughout every applied interval and for all admissible disturbances.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part 3: Optimal-cost decrease.}\par\nobreak\smallskip\noindent
Now take an optimal plan at update $t$. Let $z_{f,\sigma}$, $0\le\sigma\le h$, be the appended terminal trajectory, with $z_{f,0}=z_{H_p\mid t}^*$ and input $\kappa_f(z_{f,\sigma})$. The shifted candidate has cost
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{t+h}^{c}
={}&\int_h^{H_p}\ell(z_{\tau\mid t}^*,v_{\tau\mid t}^*)\,d\tau\\
&+\int_0^h\ell(z_{f,\sigma},\kappa_f(z_{f,\sigma}))\,d\sigma+V_f(z_{f,h}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrating~\eqref{eq:terminalcost} along the terminal continuation gives
\[
\int_0^h\ell(z_{f,\sigma},\kappa_f(z_{f,\sigma}))\,d\sigma
+V_f(z_{f,h})\le V_f(z_{H_p\mid t}^*).
\]
The terminal-cost decrease therefore covers the accumulated stage cost of the appended interval. Substituting this bound and comparing with~\eqref{eq:cost} yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{t+h}^{c}
&\le\int_h^{H_p}\ell(z_{\tau\mid t}^*,v_{\tau\mid t}^*)\,d\tau+V_f(z_{H_p\mid t}^*)\\
&=J_t^*-\int_0^h\ell(z_{\tau\mid t}^*,v_{\tau\mid t}^*)\,d\tau.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since the next optimal cost is no greater than the cost of this feasible candidate, $J_{t+h}^*\le J_{t+h}^{c}$, which establishes~\eqref{eq:decrease}.
\end{proof}
\par\medskip\noindent
The shifted-and-extended candidate provides a feasible fallback. If a suboptimal solver returns a feasible plan whose horizon cost does not exceed the candidate cost, the same argument establishes the cost-decrease inequality for the selected plans.

\par\medskip
\begin{remark}[Relation to \cite{kohler2021}]
Our formulation follows the free-center approach in Section IV-B of~\cite{kohler2021}: the optimizer selects the initial nominal center subject to the tracking-error bound. The shifted center $z^c_{0\mid t+h}=z_{h\mid t}$ belongs to the feasible candidate constructed in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mpc}; it is not imposed on the newly optimized plan. The estimation radius is initialized using~\eqref{eq:radius-update}. Section IV-C of~\cite{kohler2021} instead fixes the initial nominal center to the current estimate and establishes recursive feasibility under its corresponding terminal conditions. Here, we retain the free-center architecture and incorporate the state-dependent forcing derived in Section III.
\end{remark}
The horizon-cost decrease in~\eqref{eq:decrease} bounds the accumulated nominal running cost. By~\eqref{eq:total}, the true-state deviation satisfies
\[
\norm{x-x_{\rm ref}}_P\le\norm{z-x_{\rm ref}}_P+\sqrt R+s.
\]
Consequently, if the applied nominal trajectory converges to $x_{\rm ref}$, then
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\norm{x_{t}-x_{\rm ref}}_P
\le\sqrt{R_{\max}}+s_{\max},
\]
where $t$ denotes continuous time. Nominal convergence is an additional condition here; it is not established by the cost-decrease inequality alone.
\section{Numerical Example: Falcon Model}
\subsection{Model and reproduced offline design}
The example uses the repository \cite{rohmpc} and the saved Falcon design from 10 September 2026. The 12-state model consists of position, attitude, translational velocity, and body angular velocity, with four rotor inputs. The nominal translational drag matrix is zero in this instance. Its Jacobians are therefore independent of translational velocity; this does not restrict the controller to hovering. The velocity domain is $[-2,2]^3$ m/s, giving $v_{\max}=\sqrt{12}$ m/s.

The original 1024-point Jacobian grid varies four rotor inputs, three angles, and three body rates at two endpoints each. Positions and translational velocities are zero at these points. This grid alone does not certify the continuous domain of the remaining nonlinear coordinates.

The reproduced robust-design and terminal-cost SDPs returned \texttt{OPTIMAL}, with objectives 1494.858118 and 38.564610, respectively. The first is a weighted combination of normalized squared tightening coefficients and squared estimation radius, not an optimized contraction rate. The second minimizes the trace of the terminal-cost matrix. Solver success does not ensure useful tightened constraints: the reproduced motor-input half-width was approximately 0.1200, whereas the associated global margin was 0.4619, leaving negative available width. Consequently, this design has not supplied the nonempty terminal set required by Theorem~\ref{thm:mpc}.

\section{Conclusion}
A fixed observer certificate can support locally reduced process forcing through a Schur-complement envelope construction. Coupling this forcing to a tracking radius yields a candidate state-dependent output-feedback MPC formulation with conditional recursive-feasibility and safety guarantees. The Falcon radius experiment demonstrates a smaller local bound relative to a baseline-equivalent global propagation. Remaining work is to certify the continuous domain and numerical margins, validate the local uncertainty model, construct compatible nonempty terminal ingredients, and then implement and evaluate the closed-loop controller.

\section{Derivation of the Tracking-Error Bound}\label{app:tracking-bound}
Recall $\delta=\hat x-z$, $e=x-\hat x$, and $u=v+K\delta$. Subtracting the nominal dynamics from the observer dynamics gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-delta}
\dot\delta=f_0(\hat x,u)-f_0(z,v)+LCe+LF\eta.
\end{equation}
For fixed $z,v,\delta$, parameterize the straight-line state--input segment by
\[
x_\theta=z+\theta\delta,\qquad
u_\theta=v+\theta K\delta,\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\]
Here $\theta$ parameterizes a segment in state--input space, not time. Its endpoints are $(z,v)$ and $(\hat x,u)$. With $A=\partial f_0/\partial x$ and $B=\partial f_0/\partial u$, integration along this segment yields
\begin{align}
f_0(\hat x,u)-f_0(z,v)
&=\int_0^1\bigl[A(x_\theta,u_\theta)+B(x_\theta,u_\theta)K\bigr]\delta\,d\theta\\
&=\bar A_K\delta,\qquad
\bar A_K:=\int_0^1\bigl[A(x_\theta,u_\theta)+B(x_\theta,u_\theta)K\bigr]d\theta.
\end{align}
The domain assumptions ensure the contraction inequality holds at every point of this segment. Integrating it and using constant $P$ gives
\begin{equation}
\bar A_K^\top P+P\bar A_K\preceq-2\rho P.
\end{equation}
Define $d_o:=LCe+LF\eta$. Then \eqref{eq:appendix-delta} becomes $\dot\delta=\bar A_K\delta+d_o$. For $\ell_\delta:=\norm\delta_P=\sqrt{\delta^\top P\delta}>0$,
\begin{align}
\dot\ell_\delta
&=\frac{\delta^\top P\dot\delta}{\ell_\delta}\\
&=\frac{\delta^\top P\bar A_K\delta}{\ell_\delta}
+\frac{\delta^\top Pd_o}{\ell_\delta}.
\end{align}
The contraction contribution satisfies
\[
\delta^\top P\bar A_K\delta
=\frac12\delta^\top(P\bar A_K+\bar A_K^\top P)\delta
\le-\rho\ell_\delta^2.
\]
For the forcing contribution, $T^\top T=P$ and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality imply
\[
\frac{\delta^\top Pd_o}{\ell_\delta}
=\frac{(T\delta)^\top(Td_o)}{\norm{T\delta}_2}
\le\norm{Td_o}_2.
\]
The triangle inequality and the definitions of $\gamma$ and $b_\eta$ give
\begin{align}
\norm{Td_o}_2
&\le\norm{TLCe}_2+\norm{TLF\eta}_2\\
&\le\norm{TLC T^{-1}}_2\norm{Te}_2
+\max_{\eta\in\mathcal N}\norm{TLF\eta}_2\\
&=\gamma\sqrt{V_e}+b_\eta.
\end{align}
Combining these estimates establishes
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\norm\delta_P
\le-\rho\norm\delta_P+\gamma\sqrt{V_e}+b_\eta,
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:tracking-bound}. The calculation involving division by $\ell_\delta$ applies when $\delta\ne0$. Along absolutely continuous trajectories, the norm is absolutely continuous and its derivative is zero almost everywhere on the set where the norm vanishes. Since the forcing bound is nonnegative, the same inequality therefore holds almost everywhere in time, including that zero set.

Finally, $V_e\le R$ gives the comparison dynamics
\[
\dot s=-\rho s+\gamma\sqrt R+b_\eta,
\qquad s_{0}\ge\norm{\delta_{0}}_P.
\]
Subtracting this equation from the norm inequality and applying an integrating factor yields
\[
\norm{\delta_{t}}_P-s_{t}
\le e^{-\rho t}\bigl(\norm{\delta_{0}}_P-s_{0}\bigr)\le0,
\]
provided the estimation bound and certificate-domain conditions remain valid, as required in the coupled containment argument.

\section{Comparison of Local and Global Radius Bounds}\label{app:radius-comparison}
Consider the same nominal state and input trajectories $z_{t},v_{t}$, identical initial radius values, and the same metric, gains, and constants $\lambda,\rho,\gamma,b_\eta$ in both propagations. Define the local estimation-error forcing along its radius trajectory by
\[
q_{\rm local,t}:=q\bigl(z_{t},v_{t},R_{\rm local,t},s_{\rm local,t}\bigr).
\]
For the envelope construction in Proposition~\ref{prop:local}, $q_{\rm local,t}\le q_0$, where $q_0=\lambda\epsilon^2$ is the global baseline forcing bound. The comparison below holds on any interval on which both propagations are well-defined and their domain assumptions hold.

The squared estimation radii satisfy
\begin{align}
\dot R_{\rm local}&=-\lambda R_{\rm local}+q_{\rm local,t},\\
\dot R_{\rm global}&=-\lambda R_{\rm global}+q_0,
\qquad R_{\rm local,0}=R_{\rm global,0}.
\end{align}
Let $\Delta_R=R_{\rm local}-R_{\rm global}$. Subtracting the equations and multiplying by $e^{\lambda t}$ gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(e^{\lambda t}\Delta_{R,t}\bigr)
=e^{\lambda t}\bigl(q_{\rm local,t}-q_0\bigr).
\]
Integration, using $\Delta_{R,0}=0$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius-ordering}
R_{\rm local,t}-R_{\rm global,t}
=\int_0^t e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}\bigl(q_{\rm local,\tau}-q_0\bigr)\,d\tau
\le0.
\end{equation}
The inequality follows because the exponential is positive and the forcing difference is nonpositive. This argument permits $q_{\rm local}$ to depend on the local radius trajectory; it only uses its upper bound by $q_0$.

The corresponding tracking radii satisfy
\begin{align}
\dot s_{\rm local}&=-\rho s_{\rm local}+\gamma\sqrt{R_{\rm local}}+b_\eta,\\
\dot s_{\rm global}&=-\rho s_{\rm global}+\gamma\sqrt{R_{\rm global}}+b_\eta,
\qquad s_{\rm local,0}=s_{\rm global,0}.
\end{align}
Subtracting and applying the integrating factor $e^{\rho t}$ similarly gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracking-radius-ordering}
\begin{aligned}
s_{\rm local,t}-s_{\rm global,t}
&=\gamma\int_0^t e^{-\rho(t-\tau)}
\left(\sqrt{R_{\rm local,\tau}}-\sqrt{R_{\rm global,\tau}}\right)d\tau\\
&\le0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \eqref{eq:radius-ordering}, nonnegative radii, and $\gamma\ge0$ establish the final inequality. Consequently, under the common nominal trajectories and initialization, both predicted error bounds in the local formulation are no larger than their global baseline counterparts.
